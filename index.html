I've been working inside cultural organizations and alongside visionary founders, cultural leaders, and practitioners doing purposeful work since 2003. In that time, I've watched the same gap appear again and again — organizations dissolving before their time, work that couldn't fund itself, impact that was real but couldn't scale, deepen, or endure. A meaningful vision. An undeniable passion. Masterful artistry. And yet something essential was missing — not just unknown to the people surrounding the work, but sometimes to the people doing it. That gap has a name: stewardship. Not oversight or management — the framework that allows meaningful work to be funded, sustained, and built to outlast the people driving it. Closing it isn't about making the work smaller or more palatable. It's about building what was missing — the structure, the systems, and the organizational foundation that allow meaningful work to be held, deepened, and carried across generations. I partner with people who are building institutions and those committed to fortifying the work — both ready to make sure what they've built can outlast them. OYA Strategies exists for that work.
